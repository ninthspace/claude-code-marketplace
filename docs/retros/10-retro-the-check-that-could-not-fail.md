# The check ran, and could not have failed

**Number**: 10  
**Source epic**: 05-01  
**Status**: pending — Synthesised across epic 05-01 and quick record 15, which were worked in one session on 2026-09-21. The quick record's observation is gathered here and keeps its own origin, so what it came from still reads from the row rather than from this retro's parentage.  

## Observations

- Testing gap — The retro lesson this story was planned around turned up as a defect in the story's own tests, which is a better outcome than following it would have been. Retro 08's rule — give a rejection its own test with its own control row — was applied at the start, and the rejection still ended up unable to fail for its own reason: both it and the control closed by pinning the warning counts to exactly one of each, so every mutation that changed which rows a warning held made the rejection go red on somebody else's evidence. Five mutations were run and the pattern in the output is what showed it, not the code. Weakening both closing assertions from an exact count to *the warnings fired at all* separated them: the leak mutation now fails the rejection alone among the membership mutations, and the vacuous-claim, exclusion and one-story mutations each fail only the test that owns them. The general shape worth keeping: a control that pins an exact figure is a change detector wearing a control's clothes, and it makes the assertion it guards fail for reasons that have nothing to do with it.

Both checks passed while being unable to fail, and they got there by opposite routes. Story 3's rejection was over-specified: its closing control pinned the warning counts to exact figures, so it went red on every mutation that touched a warning and never on its own evidence. Story 5's claim was under-specified at the fixture: "naming an epic narrows nothing else" cannot be false when the corpus holds one epic, so it passed against a report that narrowed everything. An assertion too tight and a fixture too thin, arriving at one consequence — a green that distinguishes nothing.

In neither case did reading the test reveal it. The mutation did, and the mutation is also what separates the two failures: an over-tight control fails on everything, an under-built fixture fails on nothing, and both look identical from a passing run. The useful question is not whether the check was written but whether the corpus could have told it it was wrong.

And in both, the run was already following a rule it had adopted at this epic's own retro gate — retro 08's for the rejection, its own fixture discipline for the corpus. Adopting a lesson and satisfying it are different things, and nothing in a green suite distinguishes them.

- Codebase discovery — The detection this record adds already existed as a pure function and had exactly one caller — the pre-commit guard — so the answer was available on the one surface a person reaches deliberately, at the end of a piece of work, and nowhere in the hours of reading that preceded it; the cost of moving it to server open was one call site, one composer and a cheap-path comparison, which is a poor ratio against a session that spent its whole length answering from a database three weeks behind the repository and reporting a spec's seven epics as non-existent.

Neither of these is about something being wrong. Both are about where a correct thing could be consulted from, and in both the answer was "somewhere nobody was standing".

So a session read a database three weeks behind the repository for its whole length, answered every question consistently from it, and reported a spec's seven epics as non-existent. The retro-05 prediction was correct about its mechanism and wrong about its count, because the count had quietly generalised from which tables happened to carry a deep-equal read-back test. One is a right answer in the wrong place; the other is a right rule carrying a number that only held where it was first measured.

What both cost was small to fix — one call site, one skill sentence — against a cost of absence that was not. The lesson is the asymmetry: a check's value is set by how early it can be consulted, not by whether it is correct, and the same is true of a lesson. - Criteria gap — The roll-up's real hazard was not in either criterion, and the criteria would both have passed over it. A binding belongs to an epic because the criterion quoting it does, but the obvious join reaches it through the requirement — which belongs to the spec and may be shared by any number of epics. Written that way the roll-up credits every epic under a spec with every other epic's bindings, and it is invisible in a fixture holding one epic, which is what every fixture in this story held until a test was written specifically to hold two. The mutation confirmed it: reaching through the requirement fails only the neighbour test and leaves both of the story's own criteria green. That is the shape retro 07 named from the other side — a rejection whose evidence lives in a criterion that can move — arriving here as a defect no stated criterion was watching for, found by asking what a wrong answer would look like rather than by checking the answer.

- Pattern worth reusing — The sweep census this story opens with paid for itself twice, and the second time was not what it was written for. Its stated job was to budget the registration cost, and the answer — one of nine sweeps live, eight silent — turned out to be a design constraint rather than a number: `naming` pins the list of tools exempt from its vocabulary rule to exactly three names and says in a comment that a fourth is a decision, so `table: 'coverage'` was chosen because *coverage* is a word the schema holds, where `check_integrity` had to take the exemption because *integrity* is not. The prediction then held exactly, and the whole story cost zero existing assertions across 981 tests. The second payment was methodological: the first census disagreed with the pinned list on the untouched registry, because it read `sqlite_schema` where `naming` reads `authoredTables`, which excludes an FTS index and its shadow storage. Reproducing the baseline before asking about the intended tool is what caught it — without that step the census would have reported a confident number about a model of the sweep rather than about the sweep, and it would have been believed.
